Design your alloy

Pick three metals and set how many parts of each go in the crucible. The mix decides the melting range, the strength, and how the metal behaves in the mold.

    What is a eutectic, and why does it matter?

    A eutectic mix melts and freezes at one temperature, lower than any of its ingredients. It fills thin sections well and feeds easily. Away from the eutectic the metal freezes over a range and stays mushy for a while. That mush is hard to feed and tears under stress.

    How accurate is this model?

    It is a teaching model. It blends real melting points, densities and latent heats, adds a eutectic dip and a freezing range, and solves heat flow in a 3D grid of 3 mm cells. Real alloys need real phase diagrams, and real foundries use commercial solidification software. Use this to build intuition, then check it against theory.

    Build the mold

    Choose the part, then size the gate and riser. Drag the 3D view to rotate it. Click an upward-facing surface of the casting to place a riser, and click a riser to remove it. You can place two.

      The riser rule of thumb

      Chvorinov's rule says freezing time scales with the square of the modulus, the volume divided by the cooling surface. A riser has to freeze after the section it feeds, so its modulus should be at least 1.2 times the heavy section's. It also has to sit on or next to the heavy section, because feeding needs a liquid path.
