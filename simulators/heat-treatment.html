How this model works

Solution treatment dissolves the hardening phase (magnesium silicide) into the aluminum. The temperature sets how much can dissolve and the time sets how far it gets, and thick sections go slower. The quench has to be fast enough to trap it in solution, and a faster quench also means more thermal stress and distortion. Aging then lets the hardening phase come back as fine precipitates. Hold too short and the part is under-aged, hold too long or too hot and the precipitates coarsen and the strength falls. Porosity lowers strength after all of this.

The curves are simple textbook-style models fitted to typical values. Distortion is an indicative size, not a prediction for a real part.
