Powder metallurgy and sintering labBeta v0.1

Press a powder into a compact, burn off the lubricant, and sinter it. Watch the particles pack, form necks and lose their pores, then pull three tensile specimens to see what the pores cost you. A second case takes a ceramic to full density and compares a conventional furnace with spark plasma sintering.

Pressing

Density rises with pressure, quickly at first and then more slowly as the particles deform.

Results

    Strength against density

    A teaching model, not validated process software. Compaction follows a Heckel-type curve, sintering uses a diffusion-rate integral with typical activation energies, and strength and ductility fall exponentially with porosity. The constants are typical values for these powders, not your lot, furnace or atmosphere; the spark plasma speed-up is a simple multiplier. The sections are schematic. Check any number you rely on against data sheets and test pieces.
